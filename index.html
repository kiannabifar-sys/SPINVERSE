01 / THE BIG PICTURE

SMALL BALL.
BIG ENERGY.

Table tennis, also known as ping-pong, is a fast-paced racket sport played by two opponents in singles or four players in doubles.

Players use small rackets to hit a lightweight ball over a net. The goal is to make a legal return that the opponent cannot successfully return.

Mastering the game takes much more than hitting the ball. Players need excellent footwork, quick reactions, concentration, and tactical thinking.

02 / PAST TO PRESENT

A HISTORY OF
SPEED.

From a home pastime to a global competitive sport, table tennis has travelled a remarkable journey.

1880s

The Beginning

Early versions developed in England as an indoor game inspired by lawn tennis.

1926

International Sport

The International Table Tennis Federation was founded and the first World Championships took place.

1988

Olympic Stage

Table tennis became an official Olympic sport at the Seoul Olympic Games.

TODAY

Global Competition

Players compete everywhere, from school clubs to international championships.

03 / KNOW THE GAME

THE RULES
OF PLAY.

Learn the fundamentals before stepping up to the table.

Scoring

A game is normally played to 11 points. You must win by at least two points. At 10–10, play continues until a player leads by two.

The Serve

The ball starts on an open palm and is thrown nearly vertically at least 16 cm before being struck.

Serving Turns

Players normally serve two points each. At 10–10, service alternates after every point.

Winning a Point

You score when your opponent fails to make a legal return or commits a fault.

Doubles

Partners alternate hitting the ball. The serve must travel diagonally across the correct court halves.

Matches

A match normally uses an odd number of games. The player or pair that wins the majority wins the match.

Educational summary. Official regulations are published by the International Table Tennis Federation (ITTF).

04 / BUILD YOUR GAME

MASTER THE
TECHNIQUES.

Great players combine reliable strokes with spin, placement, and smart movement.

TECHNIQUE 01

THE FOREHAND

The forehand is an attacking stroke played on the racket-hand side of your body. It helps you create speed and apply pressure.

COACH'S TIP
Stay balanced, rotate naturally, and contact the ball in front of you. Prioritize control before adding power.
05 / PLAY WITH A PLAN

SMARTER
TACTICS.

Winning is not only about speed. Read the rally, choose your placement, and make every shot count.

TACTIC 01

Change Placement

Move your opponent from side to side. Place the ball wide when it is safe instead of hitting to the same spot.

ACCURACY OVER PANIC
TACTIC 02

Read the Spin

Watch the opponent's racket angle and stroke. Adjust your racket angle to handle topspin, backspin, and sidespin.

OBSERVE FIRST
TACTIC 03

Recover Quickly

After each shot, return to a balanced ready position. Good recovery prepares you for the next ball.

MOVE WITH PURPOSE
ATTACKING STYLE

Take the Initiative

Attacking players look for opportunities to use speed, topspin, and placement to pressure the opponent.

FOCUS: SPEED, TIMING, TOPSPIN

DEFENSIVE STYLE

Control the Rally

Defensive players prioritize consistency, spin variation, placement, and making the opponent play another shot.

06 / THE SCIENCE OF SPIN

MAKE THE BALL
TALK.

Spin changes how the ball travels through the air and behaves when it bounces.

Topspin

The ball rotates forward. It tends to dip more quickly, helping players attack with a controlled arc.

Backspin

The ball rotates backward. It can slow the forward bounce and make an opponent lift the ball more carefully.

Sidespin

The ball rotates sideways. Its flight and bounce can curve or move laterally, depending on the spin.

08 / WHAT YOU NEED

THE EQUIPMENT
LAB.

The right equipment helps you develop good technique, consistent control, and confidence.

The Racket

A blade covered with approved rubber sheets. Grip and blade feel affect control and speed.

The Ball

A lightweight plastic ball with a standard competition diameter of 40 mm.

The Table

A regulation table is 2.74 m long and 1.525 m wide, with the surface 76 cm above the floor.

Sports Shoes

Indoor shoes with suitable grip and support help players move quickly and maintain balance.
